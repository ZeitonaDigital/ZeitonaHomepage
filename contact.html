Contact

Begin with the work you want to understand.

Relevant conversations may involve a company project, a university or research collaboration, an institutional programme, technical exploration, or the formation of a consortium.

A useful first message

Context matters more than a finished brief.

It can be enough to explain the question, the organizations involved, what is already known, and where uncertainty remains.

A first exchange can then establish whether the work is a plausible fit, which information is still needed, and whether a direct project, research process, institutional programme, or multi-party structure makes sense.

Relevant inquiries

Conversations can begin from several organizational settings.

  • CompaniesTechnical questions, development work, and collaboration with other organizations.
  • Universities and researchersApplied research, technical exploration, and routes between knowledge and implementation.
  • Public or private institutionsTechnology contributions within institutional objectives or programmes.
  • Programmes and consortiaProposals and initiatives that require complementary participants across organizational boundaries.

What to include

Four points can make the first conversation more useful.

  1. The question

    Describe the problem, opportunity, or area of uncertainty in practical terms.

  2. The context

    Explain why the work matters and what has already been investigated or decided.

  3. The participants

    Identify the kinds of organizations already involved or expected to contribute.

  4. The next decision

    State what the conversation, investigation, or project should help determine.
